# What North is missing, and what the evidence says to put there

> Written 2026-09-10, after the owner looked at the North screen and said two things: that it reads
> like a failed Notion page, and that it would hit harder if it showed what the future him does
> **not** do beside what he does. The first is a layout problem and is answered at the end. The
> second is a research question with a real literature behind it, and most of this document is that.
>
> Same evidence labels as [`RESEARCH-ADHD.md`](RESEARCH-ADHD.md): **Strong**, **Moderate**, **Weak**,
> **Inference**, **Folk**. Where that document already settled something - the 40% doctrine, the
> refusal to score, why nothing here streaks - it is cited rather than repeated.

---

## 1. The question, stated precisely

North holds, per goal: a title, a why, an identity line, two to four *deserve* lines, and the
if-then rules that protect it. Every one of those points the same way - toward. Nothing on the
screen says what this person is moving away from.

The owner's instinct is that the away half is missing and that its absence is why the screen is
inert. The question is whether that instinct is supported, and if so, in what exact shape - because
the obvious implementation of it is one the evidence says makes people worse off.

---

## 2. Possible selves, and the finding that answers the question

**Possible selves** are a person's self-relevant expectations for the future: what they hope to
become, expect to become, and fear becoming. The construct is Markus and Nurius's, and Oyserman's
programme is what makes it useful here.

The central finding is **balance**: an expected or hoped-for self is far more predictive of
behaviour when it is offset by a feared self **in the same domain**. Expecting to do well at school
paired with a fear of dropping out is balanced; expecting to do well at school paired with a fear of
being unpopular is not. In Oyserman and Markus's delinquency work, delinquent youth were markedly
less likely than non-delinquent youth to hold that same-domain pairing, and also reported fewer
efforts toward or away from either. In the school work, students with balanced possible selves were
less involved in delinquency and got better grades.

**Evidence: Strong for the association, Moderate for the direction.** These are largely
correlational and cross-sectional designs in adolescent samples; the intervention work that follows
is what makes a causal reading reasonable.

Two consequences for this screen, and they are not the same consequence:

- The away half is not decoration. Its **absence** is itself the predictor. A screen carrying only
  the toward half is, in this literature's terms, the unbalanced case.
- It has to be **paired with the goal it opposes**, not listed somewhere else on the page. A
  separate "things I don't do" section at the bottom of North would be the unbalanced shape wearing
  the balanced shape's clothes.

---

## 3. Both halves work, and they work differently

Hoped-for and feared selves both move behaviour, by different routes. Hoped-for selves are tied to
sustained motivation and task performance; feared selves work through **anticipated regret**, which
predicts intention and health behaviour. In exercise interventions, imagining either produced more
activity at four and eight weeks than a control, and the vivid pairing outperformed either alone.

The plain reading: **the hoped self pulls, the feared self pushes, and a person with both moves
further than a person with one.**

**Evidence: Moderate.** Small intervention studies, short follow-ups, mostly health behaviour.

---

## 4. The finding that decides the shape - a possible self without a strategy does nothing

This is the one that matters most for what gets built.

Possible selves impel action **only when they are accompanied by strategies** - concrete behaviour
attached to the self. A feared self with no answer beside it is not a motivator; it is a worry.
Oyserman's academic work is explicit that it is the strategies, not the vividness of the image, that
carry the effect through to outcomes.

The communication literature says the same thing from the other side and says it harder. Witte's
**Extended Parallel Process Model** separates two responses to a threatening message. *Danger
control* is acting on the threat; *fear control* is managing the feeling of it. Which one a person
enters is decided by **efficacy**: raise the threat without raising the sense that something can be
done, and the message reliably produces defensive avoidance rather than action. The standard
summary of why fear campaigns fail is that they crank threat and never lift efficacy.

**Evidence: Strong.** EPPM is one of the better-replicated models in health communication, and the
backfire condition is its central prediction rather than a footnote.

**What this forbids, concretely:** the away half may never appear on its own. Wherever this app
shows what he does not do, the thing he does instead has to be in the same eyeful. This is the same
rule the if-then board already follows - "name one moment that takes you off this, **and the one
thing you do instead**" - which means North already contains a correct implementation of this
principle and simply has not applied it to the identity.

---

## 5. The counterweight, stated honestly

Against all of the above: **avoidance goals are worse than approach goals**, consistently and by a
lot. People holding a greater proportion of avoidance personal goals report lower subjective
wellbeing over a semester and a *decrease* in wellbeing across it; they evaluate themselves worse on
self-esteem, optimism and depression measures; approach framing produces more task enjoyment and
intrinsic motivation, avoidance framing more anxiety and disappointment. Performance-avoidance goals
predict self-handicapping and not asking for help.

**Evidence: Strong.** This is a large, old, well-replicated literature.

This is not a contradiction of section 2, and reading it as one would be the mistake that ruins the
feature. The two literatures are about different objects:

- Section 2 is about a **feared self** - a contrast held in mind that sharpens what the wanted self
  means.
- Section 5 is about an **avoidance goal** - the thing a person is actually pursuing.

The resolution, and this is the design rule the whole feature hangs on:

> **The goal stays an approach goal. The away half is a contrast inside it, never a goal of its
> own, and never the thing anybody is asked to act on today.**

Practically: the away half lives in North, where it is read. It does not reach the day view, the
Monday card, the evening close or anything that is worked through. The moment a "don't" appears on
a screen somebody is trying to get through a Tuesday on, this app has built an avoidance goal, and
section 5 says what that costs.

---

## 6. Why a bad day is the dangerous case

Oyserman's **identity-based motivation** adds the mechanism that makes this urgent rather than
merely interesting.

When something self-relevant feels hard, that felt difficulty is itself read as information, and it
can be read two ways: *difficulty-as-importance* ("this is hard because it matters") or
*difficulty-as-impossibility* ("this is hard because it is not for me"). Which reading is available
is partly a trait and partly set by the context in front of the person. An accessible
difficulty-as-impossibility mindset undermines future-focused action.

**Evidence: Moderate to Strong**, and the applied work - guiding people to read difficulty as
importance improving academic performance - is the part worth trusting least and is not needed for
the conclusion.

The conclusion needed here is narrow: **on a bad day, a screen showing a man this person is not
being is a screen that can be read as a verdict.** That is the impossibility reading, arriving
exactly when it does the most damage. The existing doctrine already knows this - RESEARCH-ADHD's
40% rule, the evening close that never appraises, "the day gave what it gave" - and the away half
has to be built to the same standard or it undoes them.

This is why the away half is written as **behaviour in the present tense** - "I don't go quiet for
a day" - rather than as an accusation in the second person - "you went quiet again". The first is a
description of a person. The second is a scoreboard with one entry.

**Corrected 2026-09-10.** The first build of this read that paragraph as an argument for the *third*
person and shipped "He does" / "He doesn't" as the two headings. It is not. What the paragraph rules
out is the second person, and the first person answers it exactly as well - "I don't go quiet for a
day" is no more a verdict than "he doesn't" is. What the third person cost was the thing this whole
window is: the owner opened it, read a heading about a man over sentences they had written as
"hate the waiting, not me", and said the app had started narrating them. A page somebody recognises
themselves in has to be in their own voice, and there is only one voice available for that.

## 7. What the evidence does not support

Written down so nobody builds them later on the strength of this document.

- **A count of days avoided, a streak, or anything that goes up.** ARCHITECTURE section 6 forbids it
  already; nothing here is a reason to revisit that. Nothing in the possible-selves literature turns
  on measurement, and section 5 says what a run of failures against an avoidance target does.
- **A separate list of things not to do.** Section 2: unpaired is the unbalanced case, which is the
  one that predicts the worse outcome.
- **Showing the away half anywhere it can be acted on.** Section 5.
- **Making the away half vivid or frightening.** Section 4: threat without efficacy is the backfire
  condition, and vividness is threat.
- **Asking for it before somebody has written the toward half.** It is a contrast, and a contrast
  needs something to be against.

---

## 8. What this says to build

Per goal, three things in one eyeful, in this order:

1. **Who this makes me** - the identity line, which is currently the quietest thing on the card and
   should be the loudest. It is the thing both halves are a contrast about.
2. **What I do** - the deserve lines, unchanged.
3. **What I don't do** - new. Same goal, beside the doing, in the same register: short, present
   tense, first person like every other line on the page, behaviour rather than character.

Side by side rather than stacked, because the pairing is the finding and a layout that separates
them is a layout that loses it.

Nothing else about North changes. No count, no tick, no score, and nothing new on any other screen.

### And the layout complaint

Separately from the research, the owner's first observation stands on its own and the screenshot
supports it: the screen has three small-caps section labels, an identity line set quieter than the
prose above it, a "1 day lived toward this" that reads as a metric on a window that refuses
metrics, and instructional prompt text sitting in the middle of what is supposed to be a personal
document. Everything is the same weight, so nothing is the point. That is the failed-Notion
feeling, and it is fixable without any of the above being true.
